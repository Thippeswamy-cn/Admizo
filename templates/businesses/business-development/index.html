Business development connects a company's priorities with realistic opportunities for growth. It starts by understanding the current offer, customers and capacity before deciding where to invest time and effort.

This division covers strategy, market expansion, partnerships and growth opportunities. A structured brief helps distinguish what is already known from the assumptions that need further investigation.

{% endblock %} {% block business_details %}
01

Business strategy

Strategic planning brings goals, constraints and operational capacity into the same discussion. A focused scope identifies the decisions that need to be made and the information needed to support them.

  • Current business position and objectives
  • Priorities, constraints and assumptions
  • Action planning and ownership
02

Market expansion

Entering a new market requires an understanding of customer needs, competition and the route to market. Work should be scoped around specific questions and the evidence available to answer them.

  • Target market and customer profile
  • Market-entry questions and research requirements
  • Operational dependencies and milestones
03

Partnership development

A useful partnership starts with complementary interests and a clear purpose. Discussions should establish the intended contribution of each party and the basis for evaluating an opportunity.

  • Partner profile and selection criteria
  • Collaboration objectives and responsibilities
  • Discussion preparation and follow-up planning
04

Growth opportunity planning

Potential initiatives compete for limited resources. A structured evaluation can help compare opportunities and establish what would need to be true for each one to proceed.

  • Opportunity comparison and prioritisation
  • Resource needs and feasibility questions
  • Progress measures and decision checkpoints
{% endblock %} {% block process %}

  • Understand the business

    Outline your offer, customer base, current capabilities and growth objective.

  • Frame the opportunity

    Identify the target market or partnership question and the assumptions that need validation.

  • Develop priorities

    Discuss possible actions, dependencies and the resources required to move forward.

  • Agree next steps

    Define responsibilities, review dates and the criteria for deciding whether to continue.

    • {% endblock %} {% block preparation %}

    Prepare a short business profile, a description of your products or services, target customers and the main challenge you want to address. Include relevant market information and capacity or budget constraints.

    {% endblock %} {% block questions %}
    Can we discuss an early-stage business?

    Yes. Describe the idea, work completed so far and the decisions you need help framing. The appropriate scope depends on the evidence and resources available.

    Will you find partners or customers for us?

    Partner or customer introductions are not promised by this page. Any outreach or introduction activity must be explicitly agreed as part of the engagement.

    What should a proposal define?

    It should identify the business question, planned activities, expected deliverables, responsibilities, timing and fees so both parties understand the intended work.
